Seasonal home maintenance schedule

Updated July 2026 by Maintley

A seasonal schedule turns a long home maintenance checklist into smaller groups of work. The schedule should help you act at the right time and preserve what was actually completed.

Spring: inspect after winter

Review roof and gutter conditions, exterior drainage, cooling equipment, windows, screens, and visible moisture. Record damage or service work instead of relying on memory next spring.

Summer: care for high-use systems

Check cooling performance, filters, outdoor equipment, irrigation, decks, and exterior surfaces. Attach service notes and invoices to the relevant equipment record.

Fall: prepare for colder weather

Review heating equipment, detectors, gutters, weather sealing, exterior faucets, and freeze protection. Use recurring reminders for work that should return each fall.

Winter: monitor safety and performance

Test safety equipment, watch for leaks or drafts, and note how heating systems perform. Keep emergency shutoff and contractor details with your home records.

Turn the schedule into home history

A checked box is temporary. A completed maintenance record can preserve the date, cost, contractor, documents, equipment, and notes that make future service easier. Maintley keeps recurring work connected to that history.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I review my home maintenance schedule?

Review it at the start of each season and after major repairs, equipment changes, or unusual weather.

Should every seasonal task repeat every year?

No. Use seasonal timing as a starting point, then follow the needs of your home, equipment guidance, and actual maintenance history.

Give every season a useful record

Create recurring tasks, log completed work, and keep the next season connected to what happened this year.
