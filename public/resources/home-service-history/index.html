How to keep a home service history

Updated July 2026 by Maintley

A home service history is the record of repairs, inspections, replacements, and preventive work completed on a property. It helps you answer what was done, when it happened, who did it, and what should happen next.

Record the work, not only the appointment

Save the completion date and a plain-language description of what the technician found and did. If parts were replaced, identify them when possible.

Connect service to the right equipment

Link HVAC, water heater, appliance, roof, plumbing, or electrical work to the relevant equipment or part of the home. This creates a readable history instead of a folder of disconnected receipts.

Keep contractor and cost context

Record the contractor, total cost, warranty details, and contact information. These details help with repeat service, budgeting, insurance questions, and warranty claims.

Attach the supporting records

Invoices, photos, reports, permits, manuals, and warranty documents explain the short service note. Keep the original document and connect it to the maintenance record.

Create follow-up work deliberately

If the technician recommends another inspection or recurring service, create a separate reminder. The historical record should describe completed work; the task should represent the next action.

Frequently asked questions

What should a home service history include?

Include the service date, work performed, equipment or home area, contractor, cost, documents, notes, and any recommended follow-up.

Is a service history different from a maintenance checklist?

Yes. A checklist describes work to consider, while service history records what actually happened to the home.
